Problem

Customers experience channels; the support team experiences fragments

Messages arrive in separate tools, agents repeat questions, booking context is copied by hand, and routine queries consume the same queue as payment failures and imminent departures. Automation without a governed handoff makes that fragmentation faster rather than fixing it.

Product thesis

One conversation core, channel-specific edges

Every supported message enters one tenant-isolated conversation record. AI resolves safe, well-grounded work; deterministic services own booking and money; and a workload-aware router assigns every warranted handoff to an eligible human with the full operational state attached.

Take the money

Passenger details captured in-thread, a hold PNR created, then a hosted checkout for bKash, Nagad, or card.

  • Name, NID or passport, date of birth
  • Adult, child and infant-on-lap fares — each priced differently

UC-03 · Manage

After the booking

A PNR lookup that answers the three things people actually ask after they have paid.

  • Is my flight on time
  • How much baggage can I take
  • Resend my ticket
UC-04 · Support

Answer the question

Grounded answers on fares, refunds, changes and Sky Star tiers — sourced from a maintained policy set, not the model's memory.

  • Retrieval over approved policy documents
  • Every answer cites the policy it came from
  • No answer, no guess — route to an agent
Deliberately out of scope for v1

Multi-city itineraries, group bookings above 9 passengers, corporate contract fares, seat-map selection, and involuntary-cancellation rebooking. Each of these routes straight to a human agent with full context attached. Narrowing v1 this way is what makes the <25% handover target reachable rather than aspirational — with one exception: involuntary cancellation arrives as a surge, not a trickle, and needs the dedicated path in B5 rather than the ordinary queue.

The one thing that must not break

Money leaves the customer's bKash wallet before the airline issues the ticket. Every design decision in Booking flow exists to make that gap survivable: bounded holds, idempotent ticketing, automatic reconciliation, and a named owner when reconciliation fails.

Channels, and what each one will not let you do

PRD §3

Omnichannel is easy to promise and hard to ship, because each platform enforces its own messaging rules. These constraints shape the product, so they belong in the spec rather than in a surprise during integration.

Full control		 Discovery and long-form search. Highest tolerance for rich UI. None imposed. Session ends when the tab closes. Native rich cards, date pickers, inline forms. Offer to continue on WhatsApp with a deep link carrying a signed session token.
WhatsApp Business
Primary		 Booking, payment, ticket delivery, recovery nudges. 24-hour customer service window. Outside it, only pre-approved template messages, and marketing templates require opt-in. Messenger / Instagram

Agent console
Internal		 Everything the bot hands over. Transcript alone is not enough context to act on. Structured state payload alongside the transcript: PNR, hold deadline, payment status, last three intents, confidence trail.

Carrying context between channels

One identity, many handles

An identity graph keyed on verified phone number, with Messenger PSID, Instagram IGSID, and web session ID hanging off it. The phone number is the spine because payment and ticketing both need it anyway.

Session state lives outside the channel

Conversation state is a server-side record — search parameters, offer set, passenger drafts, booking state — referenced by session ID. The channel is only a transport, so a thread can move without losing the booking.

Continuity is a deliberate act

Handover from web to WhatsApp happens through a signed, short-lived, single-use token in the deep link. No silent cross-channel merge on a guessed match — a wrong merge exposes one traveller's booking to another.

B2

Booking flow — the failure paths are the design

FR-2.1, FR-3.1, FR-3.2

A booking crosses three systems that can each fail independently: the airline's inventory, a payment rail, and the ticketing call. The happy path is four API calls. What follows is the happy path with every branch that actually happens in production attached to it.

SEARCHING

Slots complete, aggregator queried, offers cached against the session with a 90-second freshness window.

No availability

Offer the adjacent days automatically before asking anything. A dead end here is a lost sale that a single extra query could have saved.

OFFER_SELECTED

Customer picks an offer. The offer ID, fare family, and quoted total are pinned to the session.

Price moved before the hold

Airline fares change under you. Re-price before creating the hold, and if the total differs, show the new number and require explicit re-confirmation. Never book a price the customer did not see.

PAX_CAPTURED

Names, dates of birth, and ID numbers collected and validated. Name must match the travel document — this is the most common cause of a ticket a passenger cannot fly on.

Bangla name, Latin ticket

Tickets carry Latin characters. If the customer types a Bangla-script name, the bot must transliterate, show the Latin form back, and get it confirmed against the document. Unconfirmed transliteration does not proceed.

Passenger type is not optional

Every passenger carries a type — adult, child, or infant on lap — and each is priced, ticketed, and allowanced differently. Infant-on-lap is common on domestic routes and does not occupy a seat. Date of birth is already captured, so type is derived and then confirmed with the customer rather than silently assumed: an infant who turns two before the return leg is a different fare, and finding that out at the airport is a refund.

HOLD_CREATED TTL running

/flight/book returns a held PNR with a fare-lock deadline. A timer job is scheduled at the deadline, and the countdown is shown to the customer in their own language.

Hold expires unpaid

Release the hold, tell the customer plainly, and keep the search context alive so re-booking is one tap. Inside the 24-hour window this is a normal message; outside it, an approved template.

AWAITING_PAYMENT

A single-use hosted checkout session, amount-locked and bound to the PNR. The chat holds no card number, no bKash PIN, no OTP.

Customer pays after the hold expired

The rail can settle a payment on a session the airline has already released. Treat this as money held without inventory: attempt an immediate rebook at the same fare, and if that fails, auto-refund and notify. It must never sit silently.

PAID

Settlement confirmed by webhook, or by the reconciliation poller if the webhook never lands. Webhooks are treated as a fast path, never as the source of truth.

Webhook lost or duplicated

Every payment event is deduplicated on the gateway reference. A poller sweeps sessions stuck in AWAITING_PAYMENT past their TTL and asks the gateway directly.

One booking, two debits on the customer's phone

Idempotency protects the ledger, not the customer's perception. Someone who taps pay twice, or retries after the app hangs, may see two bKash notifications even though only one charge stands. Detect the second authorisation and say so unprompted — name the amount, say which one was reversed and when it returns. Left unexplained this arrives at the desk as "you charged me twice", which is the most expensive kind of contact to receive.

TICKETING

/flight/ticketing called with an idempotency key derived from the payment reference, so a retry can never double-issue.

Paid, but ticketing fails — the worst case

Retry with backoff for a bounded window. If it still fails: raise the highest-severity agent task, message the customer proactively before they have to ask, and start the refund clock. The PRD does not say who owns this or what the refund SLA is — that decision is required before launch.

TICKETED → DELIVERED Terminal

PDF generated, stored, and pushed into the thread plus email. Delivery is confirmed separately from issuance: a ticket that exists but never arrived is still a support call.

Invariant

At any moment, every session is in exactly one state, that state is persisted before the external call that would change it, and every state that can hold money has a timer attached. No booking state may be inferred from the chat transcript.

Kill switch — a requirement, not an operational nicety

Everything above describes one booking failing. Something also has to describe the aggregator being down for an hour. Automated booking must be disableable independently of the rest of the assistant, by channel and by route, from a runtime flag that needs no deploy. With it thrown, the bot keeps answering questions and checking PNRs, stops taking money, and says so plainly. Sessions already holding a PNR run to completion or refund — the switch stops new entries into the money path, it does not abandon customers mid-payment.

B3

Bangla, English, and the Banglish in between

FR-1.1, FR-1.2

Most Bangladeshi customers do not type in one language. They type Bangla words in Latin letters, switch to English for numbers and proper nouns, and expect to be understood anyway. Handling that is the difference between a bot people use and a bot people abandon.

Detect, don't ask

Script detection runs first: Bengali Unicode, Latin, or mixed. Latin text is scored for Banglish before it is treated as English — "kal" is a date, not a typo.

Reply in the readable form

Banglish input gets Bangla-script output, which is easier to read than romanised Bangla. Locale is pinned per session and any user turn can flip it, mid-booking included.

Normalise before the API

Bangla numerals, Bengali month names, relative dates, and colloquial city names are all resolved to DAC, CXB, and ISO dates before any external call.

B4

Payments and the compliance boundary

FR-3.1 · NFR-5.3

The PRD's rule is the right one and worth restating as an architectural boundary: no card number, no bKash PIN, no OTP ever enters the chat transport, the application logs, or the transcript store.

RailFlowConfirmationRefund behaviour
bKash Hosted checkout; customer authorises in the bKash flow with their own PIN. Webhook, with a status poll as the fallback path. Full and partial refunds via the merchant API. Settlement timing must be confirmed with the merchant agreement.
Nagad Hosted checkout, same pattern. Webhook plus poll. Refund path and timing to be confirmed — treat as unverified until tested in the sandbox.
Cards / wallets via SSLCOMMERZ Gateway-hosted page carries 3-D Secure. We hold a token, never a PAN. IPN callback, verified by signature. Standard gateway refund API; reversal lands on the original instrument.

The chat is not in PCI scope

Because payment happens entirely inside a hosted page, the chat platform stays out of cardholder-data scope. That boundary only holds if no fallback flow ever asks for a card number in-thread — including agent-assisted ones.

Idempotency everywhere money moves

Checkout creation, ticketing, and refunds all carry idempotency keys. A retry, a duplicate webhook, and an impatient customer tapping twice all resolve to one charge and one ticket.

Reconciliation is a scheduled job

A sweep compares gateway settlements against issued tickets on a fixed cadence. Anything unmatched becomes an operational task with an owner, not a dashboard number nobody reads.

B5

Handing over to a human

FR-4.1, FR-4.2

Handover is a feature, not a failure — but only if it is fast and carries context. The target is under 25% of eligible, in-scope volume after the observation window. The way to hit it is to improve approved automation, not to let the bot flounder through three more turns. Formal queue, priority and assignment rules are defined in E3.

Triggers

  • Risk-aware AI policy in E2: direct handoff below 0.45; one focused clarification at 0.45–0.699; hard-risk triggers override every band
  • Explicit ask: "agent", "human", "কথা বলতে চাই" — no confirmation prompt, hand over immediately
  • Same slot failing three times in a row
  • Any refund, cancellation, or involuntary schedule change
  • Multi-city, group above 9, or corporate fare intent
  • Any session where money moved but no ticket issued — automatic, highest priority
  • Detected frustration or a repeated complaint in either language

What travels with the customer

  • Full transcript, with PII redacted to the level the agent's role permits
  • Structured state: session ID, booking state, PNR, hold deadline, payment reference and status
  • Last three intents with confidence scores, so the agent sees where it went wrong
  • The offer set the customer was shown, with the prices they were quoted
  • Language preference, so the agent opens in the right one
  • A one-line machine summary of what the customer is trying to do
Handover during a payment countdown

A customer can ask for a human at any moment, including while a fare hold is ticking down in B2. Queueing them without addressing the timer means they lose the fare while waiting for help, then come back angrier. The rule: a handover raised while a hold is live jumps the queue, the remaining time is shown to the agent as a live countdown in the console, and if the hold lapses before pickup the customer is told immediately and offered a one-tap rebook at the re-priced fare rather than being left to discover it.

When the targets conflict, service wins

This section says hand over decisively; C1 says keep handover under 25%. Under load those pull against each other, so the priority is stated rather than left to whoever is on shift: never suppress a warranted handover to protect the rate. The way to hit 25% is narrower scope and better answers, not a bot that argues with people. A breached handover target is a planning problem; a customer trapped with a bot that cannot help them is a refund and a review.

Once an agent takes a thread, the bot stays silent on it until the agent explicitly releases it — no auto-replies, no nudges, no "are you still there". On release, the bot resumes with the state the agent left behind, including any booking they completed manually.

Disruption is a surge, not a queue

Every trigger above assumes handovers arrive one at a time. One cancelled Cox's Bazar rotation in fog season does not — it sends every affected passenger into the console within minutes, all asking the same question. The ordinary queue does not degrade under that, it collapses.

Detect before they ask

A schedule-change or cancellation on any route with live PNRs opens a disruption record and notifies every affected passenger proactively, in their own language, before they message us. The first contact should be ours.

Answer from the record, not the queue

While a disruption is open, disruption.status answers from it directly — what happened, what the airline is offering, what happens next, when we will know more. Most passengers need information, not an agent. Only those who need a decision made are queued.

Queue by consequence

Those queued are ordered by how soon they travel and whether they are already at the airport, not by who messaged first. A passenger standing at the counter outranks one flying next week.

This is a staffing requirement as much as a software one

No routing design survives a disruption without people to route to. The team in C3 builds the console; it does not staff it. Agreeing normal-day agent cover and a surge roster for fog and monsoon season is a prerequisite for the <25% handover target meaning anything — and the two seasons are predictable, so the roster can be planned rather than improvised.

Blocking

1 · Which inventory source is contracted?

The PRD names Take Trip and Implevista as examples. The choice determines fare families, hold TTL, whether re-pricing is available, ticketing semantics, and the entire retry strategy. Nothing downstream of search can be finalised until this is fixed.

Four layers of signal

The money ledger

The conversion funnel

  • Queue depth, oldest wait and breach forecast by priority
  • Eligible-agent count, assignment latency, load and timeout

E2

Governed AI agent

FR-AI · P0

AI is allowed to converse, retrieve approved knowledge and request validated domain tools. It is not an authority for fare rules, identity, refunds or ticketing state. Every automatic action is evaluated on intent certainty, evidence quality, tool state, customer risk and conversation history; a single confidence number is not sufficient.

Understand

Detect and structure

  • Language, script and transliteration style
  • One or more intents in the same message
  • Entities and booking slots with per-field confidence
  • Sentiment, urgency, abuse and explicit human request
  • Prompt injection and unsafe-data patterns
Decide

Choose the safe next action

  • Deterministic flow for booking and money
  • Grounded answer from approved tenant knowledge
  • One focused clarification for recoverable ambiguity
  • Tool call through schema and policy validation
  • Handoff with reason and structured state
Verify

Check before sending

  • Answer is supported by current citations
  • Amounts, dates, routes and names match tool output
  • No credential, internal note or hidden instruction leaks
  • Channel and language constraints are satisfied
  • Action and confidence trail are written to audit

Escalation, queues and workload-aware routing

FR-RT · P0

Escalation creates a queue item; assignment gives it to a person. Those are separate, audited events. Priority determines which eligible work is considered first. Workload-adjusted smooth weighted round robin determines which eligible agent receives it, preserving fairness without sending work to someone who lacks the required skill or capacity.

Verified identity references, channel, language, consent state, full permitted transcript, attachments, sentiment and previous contacts.

Step 1 · hard eligibility

Build the candidate set

  • Same tenant and permitted queue
  • Logged in, AVAILABLE, and not in wrap-up or break
  • Required skill certification and channel permission
  • Required language level, with explicit fallback policy
  • Active conversation count below configured concurrency
  • No conflict-of-interest or restricted-booking rule
Step 2 · fair selection

Adjust weight by remaining capacity

load = (active_chats + 0.5 × pending_tasks) / max_concurrency

effective_weight = base_weight × skill_fit × max(0.10, 1 − load)

Run smooth weighted round robin over the eligible set using the effective weight. Exact skill and language match use 1.0; an administrator-approved fallback may use a lower factor. At capacity, an agent is excluded rather than assigned the minimum weight.

What the weight may and may not mean

Base weight represents planned capacity or certified handling scope — for example, a senior disruption agent may take more of that queue. It must not be continuously increased from sales, CSAT or speed in a way that starves other agents or makes metrics self-fulfilling. Performance metrics support coaching and certification; they do not silently determine whether an employee receives work.

E4

Who keeps the answers true

PRD v2.0 requirement

The >70% first-contact-resolution target rests entirely on one thing: a policy corpus that is accurate today. The model is only as right as the documents behind it, and airline policy changes without telling anyone. This is an ongoing job, not a launch task.

What the corpus holds

  • Fare rules per family — Economy Saver, Economy Flexible, Business
  • Baggage allowance by route and fare, including the domestic and international split
  • Change and cancellation fee tables, with the effective dates
  • Sky Star tier benefits and accrual rules
  • Check-in windows, airport process, and document requirements
  • Refund timelines by payment rail — which differ, and which customers always ask about

How it stays true

  • One named owner. Half a role, but a person, not a team inbox
  • Scheduled review each month, plus an immediate update whenever the airline changes a rule
  • One canonical language with a reviewed translation — never two independently edited copies that drift apart
  • Every document versioned and dated; every bot answer cites the document ID it came from
  • No citation, no answer — an uncited question routes to an agent instead of being guessed at
  • The unanswered-question log from D2 is the standing backlog
Message templates are content too

The WhatsApp template library needs the same discipline: someone owns submission, tracks approval lead times, and knows that editing an approved template means re-approval. A recovery campaign planned for an Eid window and submitted the week before will not be approved in time.
